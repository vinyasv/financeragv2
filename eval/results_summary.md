# Evaluation results, 25 September 2026

The systems ran the same 54 prewritten questions three times each over the same 21 10-Ks (7 companies × FY2022–2024). Models and embeddings were identical: `openai/gpt-6-luna` and `text-embedding-3-small`. All vector search is exact.

- **v2:** the system under test.
- **Baseline:** flattens each whole filing into chunks, runs one vector search and makes one chat call. It ran twice: at `k=12`, about 4× v2's evidence, and at `k=3`, matched to v2's mean evidence size (GOALS.md asks for the same retrieval budget).

Raw attempts, plans and judge verdicts are in `results/`; `summary.md` is regenerated by `run.py summarize`. The Sonnet-graded baseline files behind the first table were removed to keep the repository small; the same attempts, re-graded by Gemini, are in `results/gemini/`. The Sonnet-graded `2026-09-25_v2_constrained.json` is kept because `analysis.py judge-check` reads it.

## How answers are scored

- **Numbers:** parsed from the answer and matched to hand-checked gold values within tolerance.
- **Other claims:** each question lists the non-numeric claims a full answer must make, such as dates, names, the identified segment and stated drivers. A subagent wrote them from the questions and filings without seeing any system output. They replace the earlier `answer_terms`, which let wrong details pass. In the old scorer, p02 scored correct with both acquisition dates wrong.
- **Judge:** `anthropic/claude-sonnet-5`, a different model family from the system under test. For each claim it quotes the answer, gives a reason, and returns an outcome (`stated`, `contradicted` or `missing`) judged from the answer alone. It then decides whether the cited evidence supports the claim.
- **Correct:** every gold number matched and every other claim stated. For unanswerable questions, the answer declines. An answer that merely lists every option no longer earns an identification claim.
- **Citation support:** the share of all required claims (numbers included) that are stated and supported by cited evidence, as judged.
- **Source recall:** still an exact-wording proxy for retrieval.

## Judge validation

- **The first judge version was flawed.** It passed p02 with deliberately wrong dates, 5 times out of 5. It let the cited evidence override what the answer said, and on one case its boolean contradicted its own reasoning. The quote → reason → outcome format fixed both. No results above use the flawed version.
- **Planted errors:** the final judge was tested on five planted errors (wrong dates, a wrong business unit, a wrong acquirer, a wrong target year, a wrong fastest-growing platform), twice each. It failed the planted claim in 10 of 10 trials and passed all 5 unedited answers.
- **Hand check:** 15 randomly sampled judged attempts, about 38 claims, were checked by hand. The "stated" verdicts matched in every case. Support verdicts for 5 of them were checked against the cited passages and all matched. Most of this check was done on the first judge's verdicts; it was not repeated after the prompt fix.

## Changes since the first report, and what they did

1. **Discovery constraint.** Follow-up plans may only use segments that discovery listed. p17 now uses AMD's real segments in every run; before, it invented a merged "Client and Gaming". The constraint cannot fix a wrong grouping chosen at discovery: p18 run 3 still listed product lines instead of business units.
2. **Exact-search baseline.** An approximate index had been used for the baseline. Its top-12 results matched exact search on 53 of 54 questions, so the confound was at most about 2 points. It is now removed.
3. **Budget-matched baseline.** This adds the k=3 arm above.
4. **Rubric edit made after earlier answers had been seen.** p16's two "driver" claims were merged into one: the Hopper platform shipments driver, because the question asks what drove growth. The edit was made from the question text before this v2 run. Still, v2 had answered p16 earlier, and the filing also cites a demand-side driver that the merged claim no longer accepts.

## Where v2 still misses

- **Mixed explanations (33%).** The numbers are right in every mixed attempt except one, but the prose part omits a required driver:
  - p12: NVIDIA's inventory provisions
  - p14: TI's "to a lesser extent, capacity-expansion costs"
  - p15: the reasons behind Intel's CCG and DCAI declines

  On p13 run 3 the planner asked for "Net sales", but Micron labels the row "Revenue", so the lookup failed.
- **Multi-step (33%, 3 questions).**
  - p16 names Data Center as fastest but sometimes gives only the demand-side driver.
  - p17 lists every segment's margin without saying which segment is highest in some runs.
  - p18 run 3 chose the wrong grouping.
- **Single-filing prose (78% vs the baseline's 94%).**
  - p04 misses the claim's "data center and networking" detail in every run. The claim may be stricter than the question.
  - p01 is sometimes planned as a numeric lookup.
- **Unanswerable (92%).** p22 run 1 reported Texas Instruments' total revenue instead of declining a question about its revenue from Apple.

## Retrieval fixes, measured (25 September, evening)

Tracing the saved plans and retrieved passages showed that most prose, mixed and multi-step misses came from retrieval:

- **Fragmented passages:** 63% of v2's passages started mid-sentence, and 23% were under 500 characters (baseline: 5% and 2%).
- **Narrow search:** a single 4-passage search missed the answering passage for p14, p15 and p16.
- **Prompt:** the mixed-question prompt suppressed amounts (p12).
- **No conclusion:** nothing stated the answer to "which is highest" questions (p17).

Fixes:

- **Chunking:** whole paragraphs, never splitting a sentence.
- **Retrieval:** hybrid vector and full-text search, the two rankings taken in turn, neighbouring passages, two queries per company.
- **Mixed questions:** the prose step now sees the computed figures.
- **Conclusions:** a max/min selection in Python, and a number-checked summary that leads with the conclusion.

The first hybrid version fused rankings with reciprocal rank fusion. That buried passages found only by vector search (p07: rank 1 by vector, rank 71 by full text). It was replaced by taking the rankings in turn.

All rows below are graded by `google/gemini-3.8-flash`, which is now the default judge because Sonnet was too expensive. On the 486 attempts both models graded, Gemini agreed with Sonnet on 95.6% of judged claims and 97.0% of citation-support verdicts, and it caught all planted errors. Baseline `k=6` matches v2's new evidence size.

**Improved:** p01, p04, p12, p14, p15, p16, p17, p22, n24.

**Worse by one or two runs:** n21, p02, p05, p07, p10, p18. p05 is the judge: all three answers are correct, but two are failed for saying "Apple expects" where the claim says "Qualcomm expects". Run-to-run agreement fell from 87% to 81%, so v2's answers vary more with the larger context.

**Still failing:**
- n32: the interest-expense sign convention.
- p11: strict multi-part claims, failed by every system.
- p18: the planner still sometimes picks product lines instead of business units.

**Caveats:**
- These fixes, including the switch from fused to alternating rankings, were derived from failures in this question set, so the gain above is optimistic. A fresh held-out question set is needed to confirm it.
- During this run the laptop slept and dropped the database connections. The 15 attempts that crashed were re-run with `run.py retry`. Connections now fail fast, and connection errors are retried once.

## Not yet done

- **The first reported runs** were never re-judged and have been deleted. Their 83% vs 43% came from the old term-based scorer and should not be quoted as full-answer accuracy.
- **A held-out question set** has not been written yet.
- **Scale:** the result comes from 54 questions, 3 runs, one system model and one judge model. It is a clear signal on this corpus, not a statistically established effect. Multi-step and mixed have 3 and 4 questions, so their percentages move by 33 and 25 points per question.
